## 2. Refund posture

We invented one refund case for ourselves: **a share unlock applied to the wrong book.** A parent with two
books buys the $10 unlock, picks the wrong one, and asks for their money back.

The economics are ugly. **Stripe keeps its fee on a refund** (~$0.59 on a $10 sale). A **chargeback** is
far worse: a dispute fee of roughly **$15 on top of** losing the sale — a single chargeback on a $15 bundle
wipes out two successful ones.

- [ ] Do we refund book-unlock mistakes at all, or move the unlock to the right book?
      *(Moving it costs us nothing and probably makes the customer happier. Consider it the default.)*
- [ ] Who handles a refund request, through what channel, at what SLA?
- [ ] Is there a support email that exists? (`privacy@cradlehq.app` still doesn't — see MEMORY.)

> **Cheaper than any refund flow:** make the book name impossible to miss at checkout. That's already a
> requirement on P7. This session decides what happens when it fails anyway.

## 4. Sanity-check the pricing against reality

Now that the SKUs exist in the dashboard (P0), look at them once with fresh eyes.

- Stripe's cut is `2.9% + $0.30`, no threshold: **8.9%** at $5, **5.9%** at $10, **4.9%** at $15.
- Claude costs ~**$0.002/credit typical, $0.007 worst case** — 2–7% of the cheapest per-credit price.
  **Stripe is the dominant cost, by a wide margin.**
- [ ] Confirm the live fee rate **in the dashboard** — international cards and currency conversion each add
      roughly 1.5%, and our numbers are from memory.

## 5. The free-grant abuse gap (decided, but say it out loud)

`email_verified` is read but **never enforced** — an unverified account can log in and hit the API. So
`sv2-grant`'s "first 500 signups" cap is a **launch promotion, not an abuse control**: a script with
throwaway emails can drain all 500 grants. Exposure is bounded by the cap (~$17.50).

**Decided 2026-07-09:** ship the signup grant, fold enforcement into `sv2-s14`. Confirm that still holds
now that real money is closer, and that `sv2-s14` actually gets written.
